# The CIP series template — how I write the Creator-to-Indie-Product series

A template for the long-running essay series in the Atomic Builder newsletter. Each essay follows the [[atomic-essay-pattern]] but with series-specific constraints.

## Constraints specific to CIP series

- **One product mechanic per essay.** No omnibus essays.
- **At least one number from my own data.** No "many founders..." — be specific.
- **Acknowledge what the creator economy says, then deviate.** The series exists because the standard advice fails for this specific pivot.
- **Cross-link to a previous CIP essay in every piece.** The series compounds.

## Standard structure

1. Hook — a counter-claim or specific moment
2. Stakes — what changes if reader accepts
3. Body — argument, with at least one number
4. Steelman — best counter-argument + response
5. Exit — restated hook

## Series-level structure

Each CIP essay has a *theme number* (CIP #1, #2, ...) and addresses one mechanic in the pivot:

- #1 — Why the audience step is non-skippable
- #2 — Why you don't get to skip the audience step (different framing, same point — pulled the duplicate)
- #2 (rewrite) — The voice trap when moving from creator to product
- #3 — Time allocation: the content/code conflict
- #4 — When to hire help (and what role first)
- #5 (in draft) — Habit before PMF
- #6 — Scheduling content from the product (eating your own dog food)
- #7 — Pricing for an audience that paid attention for free
- #8 — When to stop being a creator

## What I always cut

- Generic startup advice that doesn't account for the audience-first asymmetry
- "Just be authentic" — banned phrase
- Any sentence that starts with "In a world where..."
