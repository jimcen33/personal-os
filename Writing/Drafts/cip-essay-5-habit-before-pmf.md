## Outline

### Hook (1 sentence)
> Before you run the Sean Ellis PMF survey, run a habit-formation survey — because most products fail in the habit window, not the PMF window.

### Why this matters (1-2 sentences)
> If you measure PMF in customers who never formed a usage habit, you're measuring whether your product is a vitamin to people who never started taking it. The data is real but useless.

### Body (3-5 paragraphs)

**Paragraph 1 — The Superhuman playbook in one paragraph.**
Ask the 4-week customers "how disappointed would you be if you couldn't use this anymore." 40%+ "very disappointed" = PMF. Superhuman built a $20M+ business on this.

**Paragraph 2 — What the playbook assumes.**
The playbook assumes 4-week customers have actually *used* the product enough to know what they'd miss. For a daily-driver like Superhuman, this works — by week 4 you've sent ~400 emails. For a weekly product (like Threadweaver), week 4 is your 4th use. You haven't formed a dependency yet.

**Paragraph 3 — My data.**
Two churned Threadweaver customers, both from the last 30 days, told me variants of the same thing: "I tried it, got 1-2 useful outputs, forgot about it." They didn't dislike the product. They never formed the habit. By the time a PMF survey would have asked them how disappointed they'd be, they'd already churned.

**Paragraph 4 — The fix (the habit survey).**
Before the PMF survey, ask new users at day 7: "How many times have you used [product] this week?" If the median is < 2, your product hasn't entered the habit window yet. Run that survey first. Fix the habit problem. THEN run Sean Ellis.

### Steelman (1 paragraph)
> Counter-argument: Superhuman ran the PMF survey at 4 weeks and it worked. Maybe my problem is that Threadweaver isn't a daily product, and the Sean Ellis playbook only works for daily products.
>
> Response: This is right. The actual lesson is "measure habit before PMF, *especially* for non-daily products."

### Exit (1 sentence)
> The habit window is upstream of the PMF window. Measure both, in that order.
